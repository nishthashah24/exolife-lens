# Human Habitability Index Method

The Human Habitability Index (HHI) in ExoLife Lens is an educational, student-created index. It is not an official NASA metric and it is not evidence that humans could live on a planet.

## Why This Exists

Most public habitability tools focus on whether life could exist somewhere. HHI asks a narrower public-friendly question:

> What do we know, estimate, or still need to measure before humans could even discuss long-term survival there?

## Research Basis

- NASA describes the habitable zone as the distance from a star where liquid water could exist on a planet's surface.
- NASA Astrobiology notes that Earth Similarity Index-style comparisons have major uncertainty because many exoplanet properties are assumptions or proxies.
- The Planetary Habitability Index literature shows that habitability scoring is multi-factor and should be treated cautiously.

## Factors

HHI combines these factors:

- Temperature proxy: uses equilibrium temperature when available.
- Gravity: estimated from mass and radius when both exist.
- Rocky-world scale: uses radius as a rough screen for solid-world plausibility.
- Host star risk: uses stellar temperature as a simplified proxy for stellar environment.
- Orbit context: uses orbital period as a clue, not a verdict.
- Study access: rewards nearby worlds because they are easier to observe.
- Breathable atmosphere: usually unknown for exoplanets, and deliberately treated as a major uncertainty.

## Confidence Penalty

The final HHI score is reduced when factors are missing. This prevents a world with only one attractive number from looking more certain than it really is.

## Important Caveats

- HHI is human-centered, not microbe-centered.
- HHI does not detect life.
- HHI does not prove humans could survive.
- Equilibrium temperature is not surface temperature.
- Atmosphere is the largest missing piece for most exoplanets.
- Scores are for education and comparison, not scientific ranking.
